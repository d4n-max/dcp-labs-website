Leaving your pet with a sitter or family member is easier when the important details are written down before you leave. Even a trusted person can miss a feeding preference, overlook a recurring reminder, or wonder who to call when a routine changes.

Good pet sitter instructions do not need to be long. They need to be specific, easy to scan, and organized around the decisions someone else will need to make while caring for your dog or cat. A useful handoff covers the normal routine, the unusual details, the documents, and the people who can help.

## Start with a one-page care summary

Begin with the information a sitter should see first:

- Your pet's name, preferred name, and a recent photo
- Your name and the best way to reach you
- The dates and times of the care period
- The address where your pet will stay, or the location of the home visit
- The primary veterinarian and an after-hours contact
- A short note about anything that needs special attention

This opening summary gives the sitter context before they read the day-to-day details. If your pet has a similar-looking companion, include separate photos and profiles so the instructions cannot be confused.

## Write the normal daily routine

Describe what a typical day looks like in the order it usually happens. A sitter can follow a routine more easily than a collection of disconnected facts.

For example, describe:

1. When and where food is served
2. How much food is offered and where it is stored
3. When water bowls are checked or refreshed
4. Walk, outdoor, litter, or exercise expectations
5. Medication or supplement reminders, if applicable
6. Quiet time, play, grooming, or bedtime habits

Use concrete details such as “food is in the labeled container beside the pantry” instead of “feed normally.” If the routine is flexible, say what can change and what should remain consistent. That distinction helps a sitter make reasonable adjustments without guessing.

## Explain personality and household habits

Care instructions are not only about tasks. They should also explain how your pet behaves in common situations.

Include details such as:

- Whether your pet is comfortable with visitors or other animals
- Favorite toys, games, hiding places, or calming routines
- Sounds, rooms, or situations that cause stress
- Whether doors, gates, crates, or windows need special attention
- How your pet usually asks to go outside or use the litter area
- Behaviors that are normal for this individual

These notes help the sitter tell the difference between an ordinary quirk and a meaningful change. Avoid vague labels such as “difficult” or “bad.” Describe what the sitter may actually see and what response usually works.

## Make medication notes unambiguous

If your pet takes medication, write down the exact instructions that have already been provided to you. Include the medication name, the prescribed amount, the usual time, where it is stored, and how you normally give it. Note whether a dose has already been given whenever a handoff occurs.

Do not rely on a loose pill bottle or memory alone. Put the medication details in the handoff, keep the original packaging available, and explain what the sitter should do if a dose is missed or refused. For questions or unexpected problems, identify the appropriate veterinary contact rather than asking the sitter to improvise.

The same principle applies to food changes and supplements: record what is part of the established routine and what should not be changed without checking with you or the appropriate professional.

## Keep important documents easy to find

A sitter may not need every document during a normal day, but the right record can matter when plans change. Gather the documents that are relevant to the care period and explain where the current versions are stored.

Useful items may include:

- Vaccination or preventive-care records
- Medication instructions and prescription information
- Veterinary contact details
- Adoption, microchip, or registration information
- Insurance policy and claim details, if applicable
- Boarding, travel, or building requirements
- A list of trusted backup contacts

A digital copy can make the handoff easier, especially when the sitter is away from the home. Keep file names clear and avoid making someone search through a large photo library or an old message thread.

## Add a calm emergency plan

An emergency section should be direct and practical. List who the sitter should contact first, where the nearest appropriate clinic is, and how to reach you if you are unavailable. Include permission or payment instructions only if they are genuinely needed and already arranged.

Also explain where the carrier, leash, harness, spare food, and other practical supplies are kept. If your pet is lost, the sitter should know which recent photo and identification details to use and whom to contact.

Keep this section factual. It is not a substitute for professional veterinary guidance, and it should not ask a sitter to diagnose a condition. Its purpose is to reduce delay and uncertainty when a situation is outside the normal routine.

## Use a handoff checklist before you leave

The final handoff is a chance to catch missing information. Walk through the instructions with the sitter and confirm that they know:

- The first meal, walk, visit, or medication time
- Where food, supplies, documents, and cleaning materials are kept
- Which behaviors and routines are normal
- How to reach you and the backup contact
- What should be reported immediately
- How and when to send updates

Ask the sitter to repeat the most important details in their own words. This is not a test. It is a quick way to discover instructions that seemed clear on paper but are difficult to apply in the home.

## Keep the handoff updated between stays

Pet sitter instructions become less useful when they are recreated from memory every time. Store the profile, routine, contacts, documents, and notes together, then update only the details that have changed.

After each stay, ask what the sitter found confusing or difficult. Small improvements—such as labeling a food container, adding a photo of a supply location, or clarifying a bedtime routine—make the next handoff smoother for everyone.

## A simple standard for useful pet sitter instructions

The best handoff answers four questions: What does normal care look like? What is specific to this pet? Who should be contacted when something changes? Where are the records and supplies?

Write the instructions while the routine is fresh, keep them in one current place, and review them before every new care period. A clear handoff gives the sitter confidence, helps your pet keep familiar habits, and lets you leave with fewer details to reconstruct from a distance.
